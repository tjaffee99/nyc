The '''Soldiers' and Sailors' Monument''' is a [[war memorial]] located on the {{convert|366|ft|m|adj=on}} summit of [[East Rock]] in [[New Haven, Connecticut]]. It is visible for miles from the surrounding area and [[Long Island Sound]]. The monument was completed in 1887 and honors the residents of New Haven who gave their lives in the [[American Revolutionary War|Revolutionary War]], the [[War of 1812]], the [[Mexican–American War|Mexican War]], and the [[American Civil War|Civil War]].<ref name="NHPR">{{cite web |title=East Rock Park/Trowbridge Environmental Center |url=http://www.cityofnewhaven.com/Parks/ParksInformation/eastrockpark.asp#attractions |publisher=City of New Haven |accessdate=23 March 2014}}</ref> It is {{convert|112|ft|m|0}} high<ref name="NHPR"/> and 87 steps to the top.<ref>{{cite news |last=Smith |first=Abbe |title=Ikea and Wells Fargo save the New Haven fireworks  |newspaper=New Haven Register |date=29 June 2011 |url=http://www.nhregister.com/articles/2011/06/29/news/new_haven/doc4e0b3a4a55dbd480353822.txt?viewmode=fullstory |accessdate=23 March 2014}}</ref>

==History==
Interest in the monument began as early as 1878, when the New Haven-based [[Andrew Hull Foote|Admiral Hull Foote]] Post of the [[Grand Army of the Republic]] began lobbying citizens for a large war memorial in New Haven, and a site on the [[New Haven Green]] was dedicated in the following year.<ref name="Angel of Peace">{{cite web |title=Angel of Peace |publisher=City of New Haven|url=http://www.cityofnewhaven.com/Mayor/history.asp |accessdate=23 March 2014}}</ref> A committee and funds for the monument were formed in an 1882 town meeting, but objections about the high number of GAR members in attendance prompted litigation that stalled the project.<ref name="Ransom">{{cite web |url=http://www.chs.org/finding_aides/ransom/074.htm |last=Ransom |first=David F. |title=Soldiers' and Sailors' Monument |website=Connecticut Historical Society |date=September 1996 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20121217022705/http://www.chs.org/finding_aides/ransom/074.htm |archivedate=17 December 2012 |accessdate=24 March 2014}}</ref> In 1883, the monument committee advertised a [[architectural design competition|design competition]] for the monument. At least 15 designs were submitted, including one by [[Alexander Doyle]] and [[John M. Moffit]] after the deadline.<ref name="Angel of Peace" /><ref name="Ransom" /> The committee also solicited designs from [[George Keller (architect)|George Keller]] and [[Augustus Saint-Gaudens]], who each had designed several well-known Civil War memorials, but both declined to submit proposals.<ref name="Ransom" /> In 1884, the monument's site was moved to East Rock after the city council decided to retain the defunct [[Connecticut State Capitol|Connecticut State House]], causing further delays.<ref name="Ransom" />

Two years after the original solicitation, a [[neoclassical architecture|neoclassical]] design by Moffit & Doyle was selected, including an observatory, bronze statuary, and red [[granite]] shaft. Work was undertaken in 1886 by New Haven firm Smith & Sperry, with the bronze casting completed by the [[Ames Manufacturing Company]]  and the [[Decorative Bronze Company]].<ref name="Ransom" /> The monument was dedicated on June 17, 1887, with 175,000 people in attendance at the ceremony and parade, including Generals [[William Tecumseh Sherman]] and [[Philip Henry Sheridan]] as guests of honor.<ref name="Angel of Peace" />

==Inscriptions==
The inscriptions on the '''Soldiers and Sailors''' monument are as follows:

Southwest face ([[American Civil War|Civil War]] battles)
* [[Battle of Gettysburg|Gettysburg]]
* [[Siege of Port Hudson|Port Hudson]]
* [[Battle of Fort Fisher (disambiguation)|Fort Fisher]]
* 1861-1865

Southeast face ([[American Revolutionary War|Revolutionary War]] battles)
* [[Battle of Bunker Hill|Bunker Hill]]
* [[Battle of Bennington|Bennington]]
* [[Battles of Saratoga|Saratoga]]
* 1775-1783

Northeast face ([[War of 1812]] battles)
* [[Battle of Lake Erie|Lake Erie]]
* [[Battle of Plattsburgh|Lake Champlain]]
* [[Battle of New Orleans|New Orleans]]
* 1812-1815

Northwest face ([[Mexican–American War]])
* [[Battle of Palo Alto|Palo Alto]]
* [[Battle of Molino del Rey|Molino Del Rey]]
* [[Battle of Chapultepec|Chapultepec]]

In addition, the northwest face bears a large plaque with the following inscription: "1861-1865 Soldiers and Sailors of New Haven who died in defense of the Union."  This inscription is followed by a three-column casualty list of twelve regiments of [[infantry]]men.  The frame of the plaque depicts numbered [[Military badges of the United States|badges]] and [[flag]]s.  Some legible names along this border include:
*[[Battle of Fort Donelson|Fort Donaldson]]
*[[Battle of Hampton Roads|Hampton Roads]]
*[[Battle of New Bern (1862)|Newberne, N.C.]]
*[[Battle of Fort Pulaski|Fort Pulaski]]
*[[Battle of James Island|James Island]]
*[[Battle of Island Number Ten|Island No. 10]]
*[[Capture of New Orleans|New Orleans]]
*[[Battle of Seven Pines|Fair Oaks]]
*[[Battle of James Island|Secessionville]]
*[[Battle of Gettysburg|Gettysburg]]
*[[Battle of Dallas|Dallas]]
*[[Battle of Bull Run (disambiguation)|Bull Run]]
