# Ports and yards: sources (port_fill.py)

Read 2026-11-11. Licences: Wikipedia text CC BY-SA 4.0 (used only for the years quoted); OpenStreetMap land use via Overture (ODbL);
rail, road, water layers from Overture 2026-10-21.0 (ODbL / CDLA); TIGER (public domain).

## Years found
- Port Newark: first container voyage (Ideal X, 26 April 1956). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Newark%E2%80%93Elizabeth_Marine_Terminal
  The same page gives no opening year for the Elizabeth terminal, Maher, APM or PNCT (listed as 2009 operators; PNCT lease to 2050).
- 1962: "The nation's first facility for container shipping, which became the prototype, opened in 1962" (Elizabeth, Sea-Land).
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_New_York_and_New_Jersey
- Howland Hook: built by American Export Lines (coal dumper 1949); "United States Lines moved its container port operation there in 1973";
  Port Authority lease 1985 (38 years); ExpressRail 2007; CMA CGM 2023. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howland_Hook_Marine_Terminal
  The idle years 1986-1996 are the owner's brief (United States Lines failed in November 1986: from memory, not checked in a source).
- Red Hook Container Terminal: "built in the 1980s"; PANYNJ bought the piers in the 1950s; city ownership 2024.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hook_Container_Terminal
- Port Jersey: pier made of landfill 1972-76; GCT expansion 2014; CMA CGM acquired GCT Bayonne / New York 2023.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Jersey
- Oak Island Yard: "built by the Lehigh Valley Railroad and opened 1903". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Island_Yard
- Subway and LIRR yard years: `data/manual/sites/sites.geojson` (its own sources, Wikipedia).
