'''Stone Hill Church of Princeton''' is a gospel-centered, [[nondenominational christianity|nondenominational]] church<ref>{{cite news|title=News of the Churches|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19560226-01.2.53&srpos=1&e=-------en-20-TownTopics-1-byDA-txt-IN-%22Westerly+Road+Church%22----#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=26 February 1956}}</ref> in [[Princeton, New Jersey]], United States.  The church was founded in 1956 as Westerly Road Church at the intersection of Westerly and Wilson Roads.  In 2013, it constructed a new facility and relocated to 1025 Bunn Drive and changed its name to Stone Hill Church of Princeton.

==History==
[[File:Westerly Road Church Front.jpg|thumb|left|300px|Westerly Road Church]]
Westerly Road Church was founded at the initiative of two sisters, Helen and Dorothy Harris, with the support of their brother W. Butler Harris, on land along Westerly Road which they donated to the church.<ref>{{cite news|title=News of the Churches|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19560226-01.2.53&srpos=1&e=-------en-20-TownTopics-1-byDA-txt-IN-%22Westerly+Road+Church%22----#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=26 February 1956}}</ref>  The Harris siblings were the children of Princeton Professor Walter Butler Harris,<ref>{{cite news|title=Memorials, class of 1917|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=ohRbAAAAYAAJ&q=%22Westerly+Road+Church%22&pg=RA1-PR24|newspaper=Princeton Alumni Weekly|date=24 September 1963}}</ref> and grandchildren of the Rev. William Harris, a treasurer of Princeton University.<ref>{{cite book|last1=Murray|first1=James O.|title=Rev. William Harris, entered into rest, March 23rd, 1885.|date=1885|publisher=S.I. : s.n.|url=http://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=njp.32101067015972;view=1up;seq=1}}</ref>  The Harris siblings, along with many of the early members of Westerly Road, including [[Elmer William Engstrom|Elmer Engstrom]],<ref>{{cite news|title=We Nominate|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19471005-01.2.2&srpos=3&e=-------en-20--1-byDA-txt-IN-Elmer+Engstrom----#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=October 5, 1947}}</ref> were previously communicants of [[First Presbyterian Church (Princeton, New Jersey)|First Presbyterian Church]] but were dissatisfied with its theological direction under John R. Bodo.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Savage|first1=H.L.|title=Princeton's First Church|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=OxlbAAAAYAAJ&q=Westerly+Road+Church+exodus&pg=RA3-PA36|volume=LXVIII|issue=19|publisher=Princeton Alumni Weekly|date=27 February 1968}}</ref>  Their intention was to have a church where Biblical teaching and missionary activity were emphasized.  The original structure cost $30,000, raised by the 22 charter members,<ref>{{cite news|last=Johnson|first=Barbara|title=Pastor Edward Morgan of Westerly Road Church Gives Last Sermon after 24 Years of Service|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19801001-01.2.87&srpos=1&e=-------en-20--1--txt-IN-Westerly+Road+Church+zoning----#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=1 October 1980}}</ref>  and was bought pre-fabricated from U.S. Steel Homes Inc.  The sanctuary arrived via train at the [[Princeton Junction (NJT station)|Princeton Junction Train Station]] and was trucked to the site at Westerly and Wilson Roads on the border of the former [[Princeton Borough, New Jersey|Princeton Borough]] and [[Princeton Township, New Jersey|Princeton Township]].<ref>{{cite news|title=News of the Churches|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19560226-01.2.53&srpos=1&e=-------en-20-TownTopics-1-byDA-txt-IN-%22Westerly+Road+Church%22----#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=26 February 1956}}</ref>  At the time of construction, the roads were not yet put through and there was no opposition to the church's request for a zoning variance to allow for a religious use in a residential zone.  The Harris siblings arranged to call Edward H. Morgan, their cousin, as the first pastor, a position he held until his retirement in 1980.  The parsonage was built at 25 Westerly Road, which later served as the church offices until the relocation to 1025 Bunn Drive.  The first services were held on November 25, 1956 with 100 in attendance at the morning service and 30 at the evening.<ref>{{cite news|title=News of the Churches|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19561202-01.2.74&srpos=6&e=-------en-20-TownTopics-1-byDA-txt-IN-%22Westerly+Road+Church%22----#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=2 December 1956}}</ref>

[[File:Rev. Ed Morgan.png|thumb|left|300px|The Rev. Ed Morgan, founding pastor of Westerly Road Church.]]
Morgan was educated at [[Hotchkiss School|Hotchkiss]], a member of the Princeton University class of 1938, and a 1942 graduate of the [[Princeton Theological Seminary]].<ref>{{cite news|title=News of the Churches|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19560902-01.2.49&srpos=3&e=-------en-20-TownTopics-1-byDA-txt-IN-%22Westerly+Road+Church%22----|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=2 September 1956}}</ref> His great-uncle, [[William Henry Green]] spent fifty years as a professor of Princeton Seminary.<ref>{{cite news|title=Know Your Pastor|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19580420-01&e=--1956---1965--en-20--1--txt-IN-Westerly+Road+Church+Morgan----#|publisher=The Town Topics|date=20 April 1958}}</ref> Morgan had already been serving as the pastor of a Presbyterian church in Philadelphia when he experienced what he described as his true conversion to the [[Christianity|Christian]] faith, a conversion which ultimately led to him accepting the call to be founding pastor of Westerly Road.<ref>{{cite book|last=Dreisbach|first=Bruce|title=The Jesus Plan: Breaking Through Barriers to Introduce the People You Know to the God You Love|date=2002|publisher=WaterBrook Press|isbn=978-1578564354|page=73|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=RQQKEI1tYHYC&q=Ed+Morgan+Westerly+Road+Church&pg=PA74}}</ref> The number of children in the congregation grew rapidly, with the Sunday school including 85 children within two years of the congregation's founding. This led to the addition of seven classrooms and an assembly hall in the spring of 1959,<ref>{{cite news|title=News of the Churches|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19590412-01.2.108&srpos=8&e=--1956---2013--en-20--1-byDA-txt-IN-%22Westerly+road+Church%22+-ILLUSTRATION---#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=12 April 1959}}</ref> and another ten room addition in time for the church's tenth anniversary in 1966.<ref>{{cite news|title=To Observe 10th Year of Westerly Road Church|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19661117-01.2.136&srpos=26&e=--1956---2013--en-20--21-byDA-txt-IN-%22Westerly+road+Church%22+-ILLUSTRATION---#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=17 November 1966}}</ref>  The young congregation was one of the first in Princeton to offer [[Vacation Bible School]] with attendance nearing 100 children by the summer of 1962.<ref>{{cite news|title=Relaxed Religion in Vacation Bible Schools|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19620624-01.2.107&srpos=17&e=--1956---2013--en-20--1-byDA-txt-IN-%22Westerly+road+Church%22+-ILLUSTRATION---#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=24 June 1962}}</ref>  The church was also an early supporter of dedicated youth ministry, including supporting a Princeton chapter of [[Young Life]] when it faced opposition from other area clergy.  In 1964, Morgan said of Young Life that, "They reach a lot of teenagers who go to no church at all or who are indifferent church members.  They present, in a rather unique way, what Christ can do for young people."<ref>{{cite news|title=The Gang Shows Up For "Young Life."|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19640514-01.2.137&srpos=19&e=--1956---2013--en-20--1-byDA-txt-IN-%22Westerly+road+Church%22+-ILLUSTRATION---#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=14 May 1964}}</ref>  The church's early commitment to children and youth continues today.

By the time that Morgan retired in 1980, the congregation was giving 50% of all general fund contributions to mission work, including providing partial support for 28 missionary families. Most of those families were made up of those who had grown up in the church or entered it as students at the university.<ref>{{cite news|last=Johnson|first=Barbara|title=Pastor Edward Morgan of Westerly Road Church Gives Last Sermon after 24 Years of Service|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19801001-01.2.87&srpos=1&e=-------en-20--1--txt-IN-Westerly+Road+Church+zoning----#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=1 October 1980}}</ref>

Morgan helped to initiate and encourage the longstanding connection of the congregation to Princeton University by speaking on campus in conjunction with the [[Princeton Christian Fellowship]].<ref>{{cite news|title=Religious Notices|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=Princetonian19590326-01.2.32&srpos=4&e=-------en-20-Princetonian-1-byDA-txt-IN-%22Westerly+Road+Church%22+----#|newspaper=The Daily Princetonian|date=26 March 1959}}</ref>  This connection to PCF and the campus brought the future theologian [[John Frame (theologian)|John Frame]] into the congregation.<ref>{{cite book|last=Hughes|first=John|title=Speaking the Truth in Love: The Theology of John M. Frame|date=2009|publisher=P&R|location=Phillipsburg, NJ|isbn=9781596381643}}</ref> 

By the mid-70s the church had grown to 250 members and a plan was proposed to build a larger sanctuary.  However, the Princeton Township Zoning Board denied the necessary variance for parking, leading the congregation to instead plant a daughter church, Windsor Chapel, in 1976 with Morgan's son called as pastor.<ref>{{cite news|title=News of the Churches|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19760930-01.2.96&srpos=1&e=-------en-20--1-byDA-txt-IN-%22Westerly+Road+Church%22+%22Windsor+Chapel%22----#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=30 September 1976}}</ref>

Morgan retired in 1980 and was succeeded by Paul Bawden, who served in that position until 1985.<ref>{{cite news|title=To Install New Pastor|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19810204-01.2.67&srpos=3&e=-------en-20--1--txt-IN-%22Paul+Bawden%22----|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=4 February 1981}}</ref>

[[File:Rev. Dr. Matthew P. Ristuccia.jpg|thumb|left|Matt Ristuccia.]]
In 1985, Matthew P. Ristuccia was called as senior pastor.  Educated at [[Phillips Academy]] and Princeton, he received a degree in Master of Divinity from [[Grace Theological Seminary]] and his doctor of ministry from [[Dallas Theological Seminary]]. Ristuccia first joined the church after experiencing an evangelical conversion during his time as an undergraduate at the nearby university.<ref>{{cite news|last=Worthen|first=Tory|title=Ristuccia '75 forges path to ministry|url=http://dailyprincetonian.com/news/2010/04/ristuccia-75-forges-path-to-ministry/|newspaper=The Daily Princetonian|date=9 April 2010}}</ref> Ristuccia also met his wife, Karen, during his time as an undergraduate, their romance as students providing fodder for a piece in a local newspaper in later years.<ref>{{cite news|last=Melvin|first=Becky|title=Expect the Unexpected When Meeting Mr. (Ms.) Right|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics20030205-01.2.186&srpos=6&e=-------en-20--1--txt-IN-Matt+Ristuccia----#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=5 February 2003}}</ref>  

In the 1980s the Learning Center was founded to supplement the education of homeschooled students. The relationship with the university continued, including the presence of faculty members such as James Rankin of the German Department, who started his service as church pianist in the early 1990s.<ref>{{cite news|last=Victor|first=Jason|title=Rankin shares love for German, devotes time to students, teaching|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=Princetonian19961126-01.2.3&srpos=1&e=-------en-20--1-byDA-txt-IN-%22Westerly+Road+Church%22+Jamie+Rankin----|newspaper=The Daily Princetonian|date=26 November 1996}}</ref> 

In July, 2020 Ristuccia retired and was succeeded by his longtime associate pastor, Tracy Troxel.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Beeson |first1=John |title=The Impact of a Pastor: Reflecting on Things I’ve Learned from Matt Ristuccia as He Retires from Princeton, New Jersey Church |url=https://tristatevoice.com/2020/08/21/the-impact-of-a-pastor-reflecting-on-things-ive-learned-from-matt-ristuccia-as-he-retires-from-princeton-new-jersey-church/ |website=Tri-State Voice |access-date=14 June 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Staff Leadership |url=https://www.stonehillprinceton.org/about/staff-leadership/ |website=Stone Hill Church of Princeton}}</ref>

===Relocation===
[[File:Congressman Rush Holt at the Stone Hill Church of Princeton Groundbreaking.jpg|thumb|Congressman [[Rush D. Holt, Jr.|Rush Holt]] 
speaking at the groundbreaking ceremony for Stone Hill Church of Princeton.]]
[[File:Letter from Congressman Rush Holt to Stone Hill Church of Princeton on their dedication..pdf|thumb|Letter from Congressman Rush Holt to Stone Hill Church of Princeton on the occasion of  their dedication.]]
The congregation remained on Westerly Road for 57 years but by the mid 1990s the church was housing a congregation of 500 in a building designed for 125 and sought to add a two-story addition to the property on Westerly Road.  Under the existing zoning, the proposed 23,000 square foot final structure was permitted.  In response to the church's proposal the Princeton Township Committee passed a zoning amendment that would severely restrict the ability of the church to expand on its current property.  The zoning change limited churches in residential zones in Princeton Township to a [[Floor area ratio]] (FAR) of 0.125 instead of the previous 0.20.  This meant a requirement that the total square footage of the building equal no more than 12.5% of the lot, essentially preventing any addition to the existing structure.  This zoning limitation was more restrictive than that applied to [[The Hun School]], [[Princeton Day School]], and [[Stuart Country Day School]], as well as neighboring homes.<ref>{{cite news|last=Johnson|first=Barbara|title=Zoning Change Will Severely Limit Westerly Road Church Expansion|url=http://papersofprinceton.princeton.edu/princetonperiodicals/cgi-bin/princetonperiodicals?a=d&d=TownTopics19950322-01.2.113&srpos=3&e=-------en-20--1--txt-IN-Westerly+Road+Church+Zoning----#|newspaper=The Town Topics|date=22 March 1995}}</ref>  In 2004, the church attempted to relocate to a site on Princeton Pike in [[Lawrence Township, Mercer County, New Jersey|Lawrence Township]] but the requested zoning variance was denied.<ref>{{cite news|last=Alimi|first=Toni|title=Westerly Road Church Battles Environmental Hypocrisy|url=http://theprincetontory.com/main/westerly-road-church-battles-environmental-hypocrisy/|newspaper=The Princeton Tory|date=27 May 2010}}</ref>

In 2010, a piece of property became available at 1025 Bunn Drive in the northeast section of Princeton, with zoning appropriate for a church.  After a lengthy process of approvals, marked by some vocal opposition,<ref>{{cite news|last=Knapp|first=Krystal|title=Church plan grounded in controversy - Debate rages over building proposal on environmentally sensitive tract|url=http://www.nj.com/mercer/index.ssf/2010/06/church_plan_grounded_in_contro.html|newspaper=The Trenton Times|date=6 June 2010}}</ref> the congregation gained the requisite permissions to build and relocate.  A new [[Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design|LEED]] [http://www.usgbc.org/projects/stone-hill-church-princeton certified] facility of nearly 50,000 square feet was opened in December 2013.  The church granted a permanent conservation easement over the wetlands on the southern third of the site, allowing for trail access to the [[Herrontown Woods Arboretum]] from Bunn Drive.  With the departure from Westerly Road, a name change was deemed appropriate, and the church elders unanimously agreed upon Stone Hill Church of Princeton.  The Stone Hill is a reference to the [[Rocky Hill Ridge]] on which the new building is located and the significant amount of stone that needed to be removed to allow for the foundation.<ref>{{cite news|last=Karas|first=David|title=Westerly Road Church prepares to move, become Stone Hill Church of Princeton|url=http://www.nj.com/mercer/index.ssf/2013/12/westerly_road_church_prepares_to_move_become_stone_hill_church_of_princeton.html|newspaper=The Trenton Times|date=4 December 2013}}</ref>  The original building was demolished to allow for subdivision into residential lots in accordance with the zoning.  The new fellowship hall was named in honor of the Harris family, the three siblings who founded the church and donated the original land.  The new prayer chapel was named in honor of Westerly and became home to the previous building's communion table and cross.
