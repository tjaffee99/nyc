'''Princeton''' is the northern terminus of the [[Princeton Branch]] commuter rail service operated by [[NJ Transit Rail Operations|NJ Transit]] (NJT), and is located on the [[Princeton University]] campus in [[Princeton, New Jersey]]. At the branch's southern end at [[Princeton Junction (NJT station)|Princeton Junction]], connections are available to NJT's [[Northeast Corridor Line]] and peak-hour [[Amtrak]] trains. The shuttle train between the two stations is known as the "Dinky",<ref name="PrUTrains">{{Cite web |title=Princeton University: Train Travel |url=https://www.princeton.edu/main/visiting/travel/trains/ |access-date=November 19, 2014 |publisher=Princeton University}}</ref> and has also been known as the "PJ&B", for "Princeton Junction and Back".<ref>{{Cite book |last=Rosenbaum |first=Joel |url=http://www.railpace.com/store/njt_book.htm |title=NJ Transit Rail Operations |last2=Gallo |first2=Tom |publisher=Railpace Newsmagazine |year=1997 |access-date=October 9, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111003090513/http://www.railpace.com/store/njt_book.htm |archive-date=October 3, 2011 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Now running {{convert|2.7|mi|km|abbr=on}} along a single track, it is the shortest scheduled commuter rail line in the United States.<ref>{{Citation |title=Picks and Pans Review: Princeton Junction & Back |date=April 2, 1979 |url=http://www.people.com/people/archive/article/0,,20073295,00.html |work=[[People (magazine)|People]] |volume=11 |issue=13 |access-date=April 15, 2012}}</ref><ref name="NJTweighsin">{{Cite web |last=Schultz |first=Bonnie |date=June 9, 2011 |title=Arts and Transit: NJ Transit Weighs In |url=http://allprinceton.com/content/arts-and-transit-nj-transit-weighs |url-status=unfit |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121018222612/http://allprinceton.com/content/arts-and-transit-nj-transit-weighs |archive-date=October 18, 2012 |publisher=AllPrinceton.com}}</ref> Initial studies have been conducted to add a bus transitway along the Dinky [[Right-of-way (transportation)|right-of-way]] as part of a proposed [[bus rapid transit]] system.

Service on the Princeton Branch was suspended from October 14, 2018, through May 11, 2019, replaced by shuttle buses, as part of NJT's systemwide service reductions during the installation and testing of [[positive train control]].<ref>{{Cite news |last=McGeehan |first=Patrick |date=September 20, 2018 |title=For New Jersey Rail Commuters, a Bad Situation Is About to Get Worse |work=The New York Times |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2018/09/20/nyregion/nj-transit-trains.html |access-date=October 15, 2018}}</ref><ref>{{Cite press release |title=Governor Murphy, NJ Transit Announce ACRL and Princeton Dinky to Resume May 12th |date=April 17, 2019 |publisher=New Jersey Transit |url=https://www.njtransit.com/tm/tm_servlet.srv?hdnPageAction=PressReleaseTo&PRESS_RELEASE_ID=3289 |access-date=May 1, 2019 |archive-date=November 2, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191102163405/https://www.njtransit.com/tm/tm_servlet.srv?hdnPageAction=PressReleaseTo&PRESS_RELEASE_ID=3289 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Plans to relocate Princeton station 460&nbsp;ft (140&nbsp;m) south, proposed by the university in 2006 and approved by NJT and the Princeton Regional Planning Board, were met with opposition from some commuters, residents, alumni, and transportation advocates. The historic 1918 train station closed permanently on August 23, 2013. ==History==
[[File:PrincetonStationPostcardCirca1910.png|thumb|left|1865 location ({{circa|1910}} postcard)]]
The original 1865 location of Princeton station, in what became the university's Blair Courtyard, {{convert|0.15|mi|km|abbr=on}} south of [[Nassau Street (Princeton)|Nassau Street]], was replaced by the 1918 station, built on a site {{convert|0.25|mi|km|abbr=on}} further south.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Smith |first=Richard D. |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=DtLEY3S9RWQC&pg=PA50 |title=Princeton |publisher=Arcadia Publishing |year=1997 |isbn=978-0-7385-5016-9 |series=Images of America |page=50 |access-date=March 7, 2015}}</ref><ref name="SHPO">{{Cite web |last=Meyer |first=Richard |date=July 1981 |title=Summary of Nomination Information: Princeton RR Station |url=http://savethedinky.org/wp-content/uploads/dinky_historic_site_nomination.pdf |access-date=April 15, 2012 |publisher=NJ Office of Cultural and Environmental Services}}</ref> The historic 1918 station was constructed when the branch was part of the [[Pennsylvania Railroad]] (PRR) under the direction of architect-engineer Alexander C. Shand, who had also overseen construction of the now-demolished [[Philadelphia Broad Street Station]]. Designed in the [[Collegiate Gothic in North America|Collegiate Gothic]] style, the 1918 Princeton station contained a stone [[Head house|station house]], a stone freight house, and a canopy-covered platform.<ref name =SHPO/> The station has been owned by several different parties since the PRR era: [[Penn Central]] (1968–1976), [[Conrail]] (1976–June 1984), [[New Jersey Department of Transportation]], [[New Jersey Transit Rail Operations]] (to October 1984), and [[Princeton University]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=October 30, 1984 |title=PU-NJTransit Sales Agreement |url=http://savethedinky.org/a/PU-NJ_Transit-Contract.pdf |access-date=January 14, 2013}}</ref><ref>Deed between Consolidated Rail Corporation and the State of New Jersey. June 15, 1984.</ref> It was listed on the [[New Jersey Register of Historic Places]]<ref name = draft/> and the [[National Register of Historic Places]]<ref name=princehd/> in 1984 as part of the [[Operating Passenger Railroad Stations Thematic Resource (New Jersey)|Operating Passenger Railroad Stations Thematic Resource]].<ref name="NRHPnom">{{Cite web |title=Operating Passenger Railroad Stations Thematic Resources |url={{NRHP url|id=64000496}} |access-date=June 25, 2012 |publisher=National Register of Historic Places}}</ref>{{efn|name=hdexp|The 1918 station was listed as eligible for the National Register and was marked as part of a historic district in the Operating Passenger Railroad Stations Thematic Resources filing. It was not given an individual NRHP registration number. The station lies just to the south of the Princeton Historic District, according to the verbal boundary description from the district's original nomination form.}}

==Relocation controversy==
In 2006, Princeton University announced its intention to construct a new arts center, calling for the replacement of the 1918 station house, the shortening of the trackage right-of-way, and the creation of a new terminus {{convert|460|ft|m|abbr=on}} to the south.<ref name="FAQ">{{Cite web |title=FAQ:The Dinky |url=http://www.princeton.edu/artsandtransit/faq/faq-design/dinky/ |access-date=September 28, 2013 |website=Arts and Transit Neighborhood |publisher=Princeton University}}</ref><ref>{{Cite press release |title=Renzo Piano selected to design University Place/Alexander Street neighborhood |date=April 13, 2006 |publisher=Princeton University |url=http://www.princeton.edu/main/news/archive/S14/48/67Q85/ |access-date=April 2, 2012}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Hersh |first=Matthew |date=November 29, 2006 |title=It's All Conceptual, but Talk of BRT Has Princeton Buzzing |work=Town Topics |url=http://www.towntopics.com/nov2906/story2.html |access-date=March 30, 2012}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Hersh |first=Matthew |date=May 23, 2007 |title=PU Plans Still Relocate Dinky Station |work=Town Topics |location=Princeton NJ |url=http://www.towntopics.com/may2307/story1.html |access-date=December 12, 2007}}</ref> Rail advocates opposed the relocation, fearing that access to the new station would be less convenient, resulting in decreased ridership that could "threaten the train's existence."<ref>{{Cite web |date=June 21, 2007 |title=More on the Dinky |url=http://www.narprail.org/cms/index.php/narpblog/more_on_the_dinky/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070731162117/http://www.narprail.org/cms/index.php/narpblog/more_on_the_dinky/ |archive-date=July 31, 2007 |access-date=April 3, 2012 |publisher=National Association of Railroad Passengers |df=mdy-all}}</ref> The proposal prompted protest from residents, students, faculty and alumni, and led to the creation of the organization ''Save the Dinky''.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Save the Princeton Dinky |url=http://savethedinky.org/ |access-date=April 3, 2012 |publisher=Save the Dinky, Inc.}}</ref>

In October 2010, the Princeton Regional Planning Board passed a resolution supporting the continuation of train service.<ref>{{Cite news |date=October 1, 2010 |title=After meeting, no change to Dinky |work=The Daily Princetonian |url=http://dailyprincetonian.com/news/2010/10/after-meeting-no-change-to-dinky/ |access-date=December 7, 2013 |archive-date=December 11, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131211213704/http://dailyprincetonian.com/news/2010/10/after-meeting-no-change-to-dinky/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> On October 3, 2011, ''Save the Dinky'' and four local residents filed suit against the university and NJT to stop the move.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Knapp |first=Krystal |date=October 4, 2011 |title=Save the Dinky, Residents Sue Princeton University and NJ Transit to Stop Dinky Station Move |url=http://planetprinceton.com/2011/save-the-dinky-residents-sue-princeton-university-and-nj-transit-to-stop-dinky-station-move/ |access-date=April 10, 2012 |publisher=Planet Princeton}}</ref> Meanwhile, the borough, township, and university prepared a [[memo of understanding]] in which the school promised to fund a transit study and provide other benefits in exchange for rezoning for the combined arts/transit project.<ref>{{Cite web |date=September 28, 2011 |title=Memorandum of Understanding (Revised Draft) |url=http://savethedinky.org/wp-content/uploads/MOU9-28-11.pdf |access-date=April 10, 2012}}</ref>

The station house has been the property of the university since 1984, when it purchased it from NJT, with guarantees of public use. That year it was listed on the [[New Jersey Register of Historic Places|state historic register]] (ID#1742) and in a thematic survey for the National Register of Historic Places.<ref name="thematic">{{Cite web |title=New Jersey and National Register of Historic Places |url=http://www.nj.gov/dep/hpo/1identify/nrsr_lists.htm |access-date=April 14, 2012 |website=Identification of Historic Resources |publisher=New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection Historic Preservation Office}}</ref>{{efn|name=hdexp}} In 2012, NJT requested abandonment of that public use from the state Historic Preservation Office.<ref name="draft">{{Cite web |last=New Jersey Historic Sites Council |title=Draft Resolution #2012 |url=http://savethedinky.org/wp-content/uploads/hist-sites-resolution.pdf |access-date=April 14, 2012}}</ref> ''Save the Dinky'' contended that the move would breach the original agreement when the property was transferred, would cause inconvenience, and would be poor planning.<ref>{{Cite news |date=February 15, 2012 |title=Save the Dinky opposes NJ Transit application to abandon public rights in historic station |work=Town Topics |url=http://www.towntopics.com/wordpress/2012/02/15/save-the-dinky-opposes-nj-transit-application-to-abandon-public-rights-in-historic-station/ |access-date=April 10, 2012}}</ref> The university considered it to be a necessary improvement for redevelopment of the neighborhood.<ref name="FAQ" />
[[File:Princeton Station (25475256343).jpg|left|thumb|Current 2014 station platform]]
In December 2012, the Regional Planning Board gave approval for the project to proceed.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Offredo |first=Jon |date=December 19, 2012 |title=Plans for $300M Princeton University Arts and Transit project, relocation of Dinky train win approval |work=The Times of Trenton |url=http://www.nj.com/mercer/index.ssf/2012/12/princeton_university_arts_and.html |access-date=December 18, 2012}}</ref> In June 2013, NJT approved a property transfer agreement with the university, involving three parcels around the station and the proposed arts center.<ref name="transfer">{{Cite news |last=Offredo |first=Jon |date=June 25, 2013 |title=NJ Transit board of directors approves land transfer for Dinky station move in Princeton |work=The Times of Trenton |url=http://www.nj.com/mercer/index.ssf/2013/06/nj_transit_board_of_directors_approves_land_transfer_for_dinky_station_move_in_princeton.html |access-date=June 26, 2013 |quote=Under the transfer struck yesterday, NJ Transit would sell a 0.84 acre parcel located in the former Princeton Township to the university. Board]] declined a petition to review the planned move.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Mulvaney |first=Nicole |date=July 25, 2014 |title=Feds: Approval not needed for NJ Transit to remove portion of Dinky |access-date=August 17, 2014}}</ref> The New Jersey court system dismissed the 2011 lawsuit in December 2013, and a related appeal in March 2014, and a follow-up petition in July 2014,<ref>{{Cite news ==Dinky Transitway==
The [[Delaware Valley Regional Planning Commission]] and New Jersey Transit have conducted studies to develop the [[Bus rapid transit in New Jersey|Central New Jersey Route 1 Bus Rapid Transit Project]]. Parts of the proposals call for the construction of a "Dinky Transitway" along the Princeton Branch right-of-way, which would incorporate the rail service and add exclusive [[bus lane]]s and a [[greenway (landscape)|greenway]] for bicycle and pedestrian traffic.<ref>{{Cite web |year=2012 |title=US 1 Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) |url=http://www.dvrpc.org/LongRangePlan/CentralJerseyForum/brt.htm |access-date=March 30, 2012 |publisher=Delaware Valley Regional Planning Commission |archive-date=March 5, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120305002529/http://www.dvrpc.org/LongRangePlan/CentralJerseyForum/brt.htm |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |year=2008 |title=Fact Sheet 2008 |url=http://www.njtransit.com/tm/tm_servlet.srv?hdnPageAction=Project072To |access-date=April 1, 2012 |website=Central New Jersey Route 1 Bus Rapid Transit Project |publisher=New Jersey Transit |archive-date=October 14, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131014165432/http://www.njtransit.com/tm/tm_servlet.srv?hdnPageAction=Project072To |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref name="Rte1">{{Cite web |date=July 26, 2010 |title=Central NJ Route 1 BRT |url=http://www.apta.com/mc/multimodal/previous/2010/Presentations/New-Jersey-Transit-BRT-Initiatives-Go-Bus28-and-Reuse-of-a-Right-of-way-in-Union-County.pdf |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131013230233/http://www.apta.com/mc/multimodal/previous/2010/Presentations/New-Jersey-Transit-BRT-Initiatives-Go-Bus28-and-Reuse-of-a-Right-of-way-in-Union-County.pdf |archive-date=October 13, 2013 |access-date=March 30, 2012 |website=NJ Transit Bus Service: The Next Generation |publisher=New Jersey Transit |df=mdy-all}}</ref> In 2011, NJT indicated that moving the station would not be detrimental to its planning.<ref name = NJTweighsin/>

In April 2012, the university submitted a revised plan for the arts and transit center, calling for the extension of the station's freight house onto the right-of-way for possible use as a restaurant. The Regional Planning Board introduced an ordinance requiring the land be preserved for a transportation right-of-way that could eventually extend farther into the central business district at Nassau Street. According to the university, ownership of the trackage would have to change hands in order for the extended transitway to be implemented.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Clerkin |first=Bridget |date=April 25, 2012 |title=Princeton University submits revised plan for $300M arts and transit center |work=The Times of Trenton |url=http://www.nj.com/mercer/index.ssf/2012/04/princetion_university_submits.html |access-date=April 25, 2012}}</ref> Approvals were subsequently issued for converting the station house and the expanded freight house into a pair of restaurants, preserving two original ticket windows for kitchen views, and using the wooden arrival/departure boards as overhead menus.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Tanner |first=Pat |date=September 2016 |title=Jim Nawn has big plans for Dinky Bar & Kitchen |work=Princeton Echo |url=http://allprinceton.com/content/arts-and-transit-nj-transit-weighs |url-status=bot: unknown |access-date=September 25, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121018222612/http://allprinceton.com/content/arts-and-transit-nj-transit-weighs |archive-date=October 18, 2012 |df=mdy-all}}</ref> The Dinky Bar & Kitchen opened in the old station in 2016.<ref>[https://www.dinkybarandkitchen.com/about/ About | Dinky Bar & Kitchen]</ref>
