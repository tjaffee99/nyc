The '''Joseph Henry House''' is a historic building located on the campus of [[Princeton University]] in [[Princeton, New Jersey|Princeton]], [[Mercer County, New Jersey|Mercer County]], [[New Jersey]], United States. [[Joseph Henry]], a prominent American [[physicist]] who worked in [[electromagnetics]], designed the house in 1836 and lived there from its completion in 1838 until taking a position as the first secretary of the [[Smithsonian Institution]] in 1848.<ref>[http://etcweb.princeton.edu/CampusWWW/Companion/joseph_henry_house.html "Joseph Henry House" in Alexander Leitch, ''A Princeton Companion'' (1978).]</ref><ref name="Princeton Campus Building Profile">[http://etcweb.princeton.edu/scripts/perry/bldgInfo.pl?id=481 Princeton Campus Building Profile]</ref> The construction of the house was offered to the young physicist as part of the University's attempt to hire him away from the [[Albany Academy]] in an attempt to raise Princeton's profile.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://etcweb.princeton.edu/CampusWWW/Companion/henry_joseph.html |title="Joseph Henry" in Alexander Leitch, ''A Princeton Companion'' (1978). |access-date=2012-02-18 |archive-date=2011-08-12 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110812130355/http://etcweb.princeton.edu/CampusWWW/Companion/henry_joseph.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> After Henry's departure, the house served as the official housing of the Dean of the College, the University's senior undergraduate academic officer, from 1909 to 1961.

The Henry house has been moved repeatedly throughout its history, first in 1870 to a site behind East College, again in 1925 to the corner of Washington Road and Nassau Street to accommodate the construction of the [[Princeton University Chapel]], and finally in 1946 to its present location.<ref name="Princeton Campus Building Profile"/> The Henry house now stands along the northern edge of the University's campus adjacent to Chancellor Green and serves the home of Princeton's Adlinger Center for the Humanities.<ref>[http://humanities.princeton.edu/ Council of the Humanities]</ref> The house was declared a National Historic Landmark in 1965.<ref>[http://tps.cr.nps.gov/nhl/detail.cfm?ResourceID=327&resourceType=Building National Historic Landmarks Program] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090225015130/http://tps.cr.nps.gov/nhl/detail.cfm?ResourceId=327&ResourceType=Building |date=2009-02-25 }}</ref><ref>[http://www.hmdb.org/marker.asp?marker=44814 Historical Marker Database]</ref>

The house is a {{frac|2|1|2}}-story brick building with a gabled roof.  It has an asymmetrical five-bay facade, with a single-story open porch extending across the front and single-story wings extending to the sides.  The roof is pierced by several irregularly placed chimneys.  The main entrance is roughly centered on the facade, with a three-light transom window above.<ref name=nhlnom>{{cite web|url={{NHLS url|66000464}}|title=NHL nomination for Joseph Henry House|publisher=National Park Service|access-date=2020-04-15}}</ref>
