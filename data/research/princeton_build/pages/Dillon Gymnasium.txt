'''Dillon Gymnasium''' is an on-campus multi-purpose athletic facility on the campus of [[Princeton University]] in [[Princeton, New Jersey]].  It was built in 1947 to replace University Gymnasium, which had burned to the ground in 1944.  It houses a 1,500-seat gymnasium, squash courts and a pool.

From 1947 to 1969 it housed the men's basketball team before the building of [[Jadwin Gymnasium]].  It currently houses the men's and women's volleyball teams and the wrestling team.  It is named for Herbert L. Dillon, class of 1907, a one-time football captain and a principal donor to the building which bears his name.<ref>{{cite book|last1=Leitch|first1=Alexander|title=A Princeton Companion|date=1978|publisher=Princeton University Press|page=137|url=http://etcweb.princeton.edu/CampusWWW/Companion/dillon_gymnasium.html}}</ref>

As the headquarters of [[Princeton University]]'s Campus Recreation program, Dillon is also home to the {{convert|8000|sqft|m2|adj=on}} '''Stephens Fitness Center''', a {{convert|1280|sqft|m2|adj=on}} Dance Studio, a {{convert|1250|sqft|m2|adj=on}} Martial Arts Room, and a Spinning Room with 14 bikes.<ref>{{cite web|title=Dillon Gym|url=https://www.princeton.edu/campusrec/dillon-gym/description-of-facilities/Description-Dillon.pdf|website=Campus Recreation|publisher=Princeton University|access-date=2016-03-31|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160413220348/https://www.princeton.edu/campusrec/dillon-gym/description-of-facilities/Description-Dillon.pdf|archive-date=2016-04-13|url-status=dead}}</ref>

On 15 February 1964 [[Bill Bradley]] scored 51 points here in a basketball game for Princeton University, allowing Princeton to defeat [[Harvard University]], 87-56, before 2,700 fans.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1964/02/16/archives/bradley-scores-51-as-princeton-wins.html|title=Bradley Scores 51 as Princeton Wins|newspaper=The New York Times|date=16 February 1964}}</ref>
