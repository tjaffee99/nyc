'''The Palmer Memorial Stadium''' was a [[stadium]] in [[Princeton, New Jersey]], United States, owned operated by [[Princeton University]]. It served as the home venue for [[Princeton Tigers football]] team as well as Princeton's [[track and field]] team.<ref>{{cite web|last=Levin|first=Anne|url=http://www.princetonmagazine.com/palmer-stadium-memories-of-a-magical-time/|title=Palmer Stadium – Memories Of A Magical Time|work=Princeton Magazine}}</ref> The stadium was opened on October 24, 1914 with the [[1914 Princeton Tigers football team]]'s game against [[1914 Dartmouth football team|Dartmouth]], and held 45,750 people at its peak. It closed in 1996 with the [[1996 Princeton Tigers football team]]'s season finale. [[Princeton Stadium]] was built on the site, albeit pushed slightly further north, in 1997.

The building was named for Stephen S. Palmer, a trustee of the university, by his son, Edgar Palmer III. Like [[Harvard Stadium]], it was horseshoe-shaped (which was modeled after the Greek Olympic Stadium), but was wider, including a full-sized track (around the football field). It opened to the south (facing [[Lake Carnegie (New Jersey)|Lake Carnegie]]) and the grand main entrance was at the north.

Palmer Stadium hosted the [[New York Giants]] of the [[National Football League]] (NFL) for one exhibition game per season from 1965 to 1975, the first ten games against the [[Philadelphia Eagles]] and then the [[Pittsburgh Steelers]] in 1975. It hosted the championship game of the [[1981 NCAA Division I men's lacrosse tournament]]. From 1936 to its closing, the track's long-jump record was held by [[Jesse Owens]].
