'''Bendheim Center for Finance''' ('''BCF''') is an [[interdisciplinary]] center at [[Princeton University]]. It was established in 1997 at the initiative of [[Ben Bernanke]]. [[Yacine Ait-Sahalia]] served as the center's inaugural director (1998–2014). The center is dedicated to research and education in the area of money and [[finance]], in lieu of there not being a full professional [[business school]] at Princeton.<ref>{{cite web|title=About the Bendheim Center|url=https://www.princeton.edu/bcf/about/|publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance}}</ref>

==Faculty==

==Research==
The Bendheim Center for Finance obtained academic reputation within few years, benefiting from its faculty's focus on financial imperfections and [[Behavioral economics|behavioral]] elements which experienced a boost in public interest and academic research after the burst of the [[dot-com bubble]] in 2000. Similarly, the faculty's empirical research on housing and theories incorporating financial frictions and the financial sector into macro and monetary models contributed to the Bendheim Center's reputation as these are at the heart of the rethinking of [[macroeconomics]], [[monetary economics]], [[financial regulation]], and the [[international financial architecture]] triggered by the great recession.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB121089412378097011 |title=Bernanke's Bubble Laboratory |access-date=July 24, 2008 |publisher=The Wall Street Journal |date=May 16, 2008 |author=Justin Lahart}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://blogs.wsj.com/deals/2010/09/02/ben-bernankes-labor-day-reading-list/ |title=Ben Bernanke's Labor Day Reading List |access-date=September 3, 2010 |publisher=The Wall Street Journal |date=September 2, 2010 |author=Michael Corkery}}</ref>

==Academic programs==
The Bendheim Center for Finance offers teaching and supervision to [[Undergraduate education|undergraduate]] and [[Postgraduate education|graduate]] students. The center has close ties with [[Princeton University Department of Economics|Princeton's Department of Economics]].

===Undergraduate certificate in finance===
[[Princeton University]]'s [[Undergraduate education|undergraduate]] students of all departments can earn a [[Minor (academic)|certificate]] attesting their proficiency in the area of [[finance]] from the Bendheim Center.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/undergraduate-certificate-program/ |title=Undergraduate Certificate in Finance |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180131143408/https://bcf.princeton.edu/undergraduate-certificate-program/ |archive-date=2018-01-31 |url-status=dead }}</ref> Prerequisites for admission focus on skills in [[mathematics]], [[probability theory]] and [[statistics]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/undergraduate-certificate-program/admission/ |title=Admission to the Undergraduate Certificate in Finance |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance |archive-date=October 8, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171008140308/https://bcf.princeton.edu/undergraduate-certificate-program/admission/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> The two core courses deal with [[asset pricing]] and [[corporate finance]]. The elective courses cover areas such as [[behavioral finance]], [[data science]], [[portfolio theory]], money and banking, [[financial crisis|financial crises]], [[risk management]], [[information technologies]] for [[finance]], [[public policy]], [[ethics]] in [[finance]], and the Chinese [[economic system|economic]] and [[financial system]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/undergraduate-certificate-program/courses/ |title=Undergraduate Certificate in Finance Courses |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance |archive-date=October 7, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171007200951/https://bcf.princeton.edu/undergraduate-certificate-program/courses/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> Undergraduate students also have the opportunity to take part in the BCF's mentorship program, which connects students and [[alumni]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/undergraduate-certificate-program/mentorship-placement/ |title=Undergraduate Mentorship and Placement |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170713145414/http://bcf.princeton.edu/undergraduate-certificate-program/mentorship-placement/ |archive-date=2017-07-13 |url-status=dead }}</ref> Currently, around 90 students take part in the undergraduate certificate in finance program.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/undergraduate-certificate-program/alumni/ |title=Undergraduate Certificate in Finance Alumni |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance}}</ref> While approximately two thirds of the students have an [[economics]] or [[finance]] background, many come from other areas including [[computer science]], [[history]], [[mathematics]], and [[engineering]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/undergraduate-certificate-program/current-students/ |title=Undergraduate Certificate in Finance Students |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance |archive-date=October 29, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171029145635/http://bcf.princeton.edu/undergraduate-certificate-program/current-students/ |url-status=dead }}</ref>

===Master in finance===
The [[master in finance]] program particularly addresses students aiming at a career in [[quantitative finance]] and [[asset management]], [[risk management]], [[financial technology|FinTechs]], macroeconomic and financial [[forecasting]], quantitative trading, or applied research. While the program generally takes four semesters, a two-semester version is available to students with strong prior knowledge.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/master-in-finance/ |title=Master in Finance |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance}}</ref> The BCF's Master in Finance puts strong emphasis on [[financial economics]], [[data analysis]] and technology, and computational methods.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/master-in-finance/courses/ |title=Master in Finance Courses |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance |archive-date=October 8, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171008142007/https://bcf.princeton.edu/master-in-finance/courses/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> Due to the quantitative focus of the courses, the master program is also attractive for students with strong corresponding prior knowledge such as [[physicist]]s.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.science.org/content/article/physicists-learn-trade |title=Physicists Learn a Trade |author=Michael Price |date=August 3, 2012 |publisher=Science Magazine |access-date=January 29, 2018}}</ref> Approximately 35 students corresponding to 5 percent of applicants are admitted to the program each year.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/master-in-finance/recruiting-placement/ |title=Master in Finance Recruiting |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171014070622/http://bcf.princeton.edu/master-in-finance/recruiting-placement/ |archive-date=2017-10-14 |url-status=dead }}</ref> The ''[[New York Sun]]'' describes the program as a popular alternative to an [[MBA]], particularly for students striving for a career in trading or in financial companies.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.nysun.com/article/34738 |title=Princeton's Finance Program A Popular Alternative to MBAs |author=Liz Peek |date=June 20, 2006 |publisher=The New York Sun |access-date=March 8, 2017 |author-link=Liz Peek |archive-date=August 21, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080821220648/http://www.nysun.com/article/34738 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

===Ph.D. program===
The BCF does not offer a distinct [[Doctor of Philosophy|Ph.D. program]], but provides a home for [[Doctor of Philosophy|Ph.D. students]] in [[economics]], [[operations research]], [[mathematics]], and further fields who are interested in finance-related topics.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/phd/ |title=For Ph.D. Students |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance}}</ref> In addition to supervision, the BCF offers courses on [[finance]] and related topics.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/phd/courses/ |title=Ph.D. Courses |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance |archive-date=August 24, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170824192634/https://bcf.princeton.edu/phd/courses/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> The center also hosts a weekly student research workshop.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/phd/student-research-workshop/ |title=Student Research Workshop |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance |archive-date=August 24, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170824193927/https://bcf.princeton.edu/phd/student-research-workshop/ |url-status=dead }}</ref>

==Seminars, lecture series, and conferences==
In addition to the student research workshop, the BCF hosts a weekly finance seminar. The center organizes the "Princeton Finance Lectures" delivered by one outstanding [[finance]] scholar each year. In the past years, lectures were given by [[Raghuram Rajan]], [[Jeremy Stein]], [[Ben Bernanke]], [[Andrei Shleifer]], [[Robert Shiller]], [[Robert Engle]], [[Robert C. Merton]], and [[Lars Peter Hansen]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/seminars/ |title=Seminars |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance |archive-date=October 15, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171015043249/https://bcf.princeton.edu/seminars/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> The BCF regularly hosts conferences. Past conferences dealt with topics such as escalating risks in China and [[Financial technology|FinTech]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/conferences/ |title=Conferences |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance |archive-date=October 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171009104617/https://bcf.princeton.edu/conferences/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> Each year, the BCF co-organizes the "Princeton Initiative: Macro, Money and Finance", which brings together about 75 Ph.D. students from various universities to explore the latest research at the intersection between [[macroeconomics|macro]], [[monetary economics|monetary]], and [[financial economics]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/initiatives/ |title=Initiatives |access-date=January 23, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance}}</ref> In the center's career speaker series, guests from industry, government, and academia cover a broad range of topics, addressing both graduate and undergraduate students.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://bcf.princeton.edu/career-services-events/ |title=Career Speaker Series |access-date=January 29, 2018 |publisher=Bendheim Center for Finance |archive-date=October 18, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171018120952/https://bcf.princeton.edu/career-services-events/ |url-status=dead }}</ref>
