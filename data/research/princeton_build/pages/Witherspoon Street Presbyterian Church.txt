'''Witherspoon Street Presbyterian Church''' was founded in 1839 in [[Princeton, New Jersey|Princeton]], [[New Jersey]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|title=A Reckoning in Princeton|url=https://www.history.pcusa.org/blog/2016/02/reckoning-princeton|website=Presbyterian Historical Society|access-date=2020-05-11}}</ref>  The church was formed after the [[Nassau Presbyterian Church]] allowed 90 of the 131 former [[African Americans|African American]] members to form their own church, after a fire had devastated the Nassau church. The church is among New Jersey's oldest African American [[Presbyterianism|Presbyterian]] congregations.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web|title=Witherspoon Street Church (1836– )|url=https://www.blackpast.org/african-american-history/witherspoon-street-church-1836/|last=Nielsen|first=Euell A.|date=2016-11-30|language=en-US|access-date=2020-05-11}}</ref>

The sanctuary was built in 1840 in the [[Greek Revival architecture|Greek Revival]] style. The recessed entrance was a new and common design feature on religious architecture of the time.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Witherspoon Street Presbyterian Church|url=https://www.princetonnj.gov/properties/witherspoon-street-presbyterian-church|website=www.princetonnj.gov|language=en|access-date=2020-05-11}}</ref> The church was first called the "First Presbyterian Church of Color of Princeton", but reported to the [[Presbyterian polity|General Assembly]] in 1845 as the "Witherspoon Street Presbyterian Church".<ref name=":0" />

[[Betsey Stockton]], who may have been the first black Presbyterian missionary in the U.S., helped found the church after she returned to Princeton in 1835 from her work as a missionary in [[Hawaii]].<ref name=":1" /> [[William Drew Robeson I|Reverend William Drew Robeson]] led the church as pastor from 1879. Robeson moved into the church [[Clergy house|parsonage]] with his wife [[Maria Louisa Bustill]] of [[Philadelphia]], [[Pennsylvania]], and there they raised their children, including [[Paul Robeson]]. Reverend Robeson was a former slave, and as such he preached racial equality, which eventually led to his forced resignation in 1901 for being "too radical."<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":1" /> [[Susie Ione Brown Waxwood]], a YWCA official and local clubwoman, joined Witherspoon in 1942; she was an ordained elder and president of the Women's Association.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Oppenheim|first=Linda|date=2017-09-13|title=Happy Birthday, Mrs. Waxwood!|url=https://niotprinceton.org/2017/09/13/happy-birthday-mrs-waxwood/|access-date=2022-02-09|website=Not In Our Town Princeton|language=en}}</ref>

In 2018, the church installed a new pastor, Reverend Lukata Mjumbe, a graduate of the [[Princeton Theological Seminary]].<ref>{{Cite web|title=Witherspoon Street Presbyterian Church names new pastor|url=https://planetprinceton.com/2018/11/26/witherspoon-street-presbyterian-church-names-new-pastor/|last=Knapp|first=Krystal|date=2018-11-27|website=Planet Princeton|language=en|access-date=2020-05-11}}</ref>
